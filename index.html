Citation Verification Tool

This tool uses open scholarly indexes, such as CrossRef and OpenAlex, to help verify cited resources.

This is not to be assumed as a definitive tool, nor used to evaluate work. It is intended to help users locate and confirm cited resources.

Not finding a resource with this tool does not confirm the resource does not exist. It only confirms that the open scholarly indexes used in this tool did not locate it.

What this tool is — and isn't

What it is

  • A way to look up a citation in several open scholarly indexes and see the published record.
  • A cross-check: it reports the title, journal, authors, and year those indexes hold.
  • A first-pass signal — a confidence score, a retraction flag, and a comparison of details you enter against the record.
  • Powered by real database records, not generative AI — every result shown is an actual entry in a named index, never text a model produced.
  • An aid to your own judgment, meant to speed up investigating a citation you have questions about.

What it is not

  • Not a plagiarism or misconduct detector, and not a judgment on anyone's work or integrity.
  • Not an AI or language model — it does not generate, summarize, or invent citations, so it cannot hallucinate a source that isn't there.
  • Not proof a source does or does not exist — only a report of what these indexes contain.
  • Not comprehensive: it misses web-only sources, gray literature, many book chapters, and anything unindexed.
  • Not confirmation that a found record is the work actually cited — matching is probabilistic; you confirm it.
  • Not a retraction authority — the absence of a flag does not guarantee a work is in good standing.

Title Search

Paste a full citation, enter a DOI for an exact match, or fill specific fields. This returns the published title, journal, and link, checks any author/year/journal you enter against the record, and flags retractions.

A pasted reference returns a best-guess match. The tool can't confirm it's the same work you pasted — check the returned title, authors, and year against your source before trusting it.

Matching is approximate. A "weak match" flag means the result fell below the confidence bar, and a pasted reference always returns a best guess — confirm the title, authors, and year against your source before citing.
Sources: a DOI is resolved directly against CrossRef and OpenAlex for an exact match. Otherwise CrossRef and OpenAlex are queried first; Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC, and the book indexes Google Books and Open Library are checked only when neither returns a strong match. Free full-text links come from Unpaywall, DOAJ indicates whether the journal is a recognized open-access title, and retraction flags come from CrossRef and OpenAlex metadata. All are open and keyless.

Journal Search

Enter the journal and volume, including the issue, (if available). This returns all indexed articles with page ranges for the journal volume, sourced from OpenAlex.

Book Search

Enter a book or edited-volume title, or an ISBN for an exact lookup. This confirms the volume in Google Books and Open Library and lists its publisher, editors, and a link.

This confirms the book or edited volume, not an individual chapter within it. A found book does not verify that a specific cited chapter, its author, or its page range exist.

Book records come from Google Books and Open Library, both open and keyless. An ISBN gives an exact match; a title search is approximate. This confirms the volume, not a chapter within it.
